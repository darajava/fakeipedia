# Hellenic Empire

## World brief

You are building an alternate history in which Alexander's empire never fragmented and an unbroken Hellenic state has survived into 2026. Alexander recovers from his illness in Babylon, establishes a workable succession and binds the Greek cities and conquered provinces into a durable imperial system. Its survival comes through reforms, compromises and occasional brutal repression, rather than an implausible absence of crises.

Artificial intelligence does not exist in this world. Do not introduce AI, machine intelligence or an equivalent under another name. Technology and institutions are developed and operated by people.

## Featured article and scope

The Hellenic Empire is the main entry point into its own wiki. Treat the target as a flagship featured article of approximately **2,500–3,500 words**, with a substantial introduction, **8–10 developed sections**, useful subsections and a concluding See also section.

Write an encyclopaedic account of the empire as it exists in 2026. Establish its official name, borders, population, capital, government and relationship with independent states. It remains recognisably Greek after more than two millennia, but its institutions and culture have changed profoundly. It does not rule the entire planet.

Include a history section and a selective timeline from Alexander's reign to the present. Explain how succession disputes, provincial revolts, epidemics, religious movements and industrialisation tested the state without permanently breaking it apart. Give the empire distinct historical periods, memorable rulers, constitutional settlements and contested turning points.

Explain what became of Rome and how a surviving Hellenic power changed the Mediterranean, Persia, Egypt and relations with India and China. Develop a coherent wider world rather than reproducing modern countries, wars and colonial empires unchanged. Establish which territories remain imperial provinces, which gained autonomy and which became independent.

Describe the balance between the imperial throne, a common assembly, provincial administrations and the self-government of the poleis. Explore the tension between Greek civic ideals and imperial rule. Explain who historically qualified as a citizen, how slavery ended, how women's rights changed and which inequalities remain in 2026. Avoid treating ancient democracy as universal or the empire's endurance as proof of benevolence.

Give religion and philosophy substantial attention. Show how worship of the Olympian gods evolved, how temples function today and how other faiths developed within or beyond the empire. Describe contemporary philosophical schools, public arguments and the relationship between religious authority, science and government.

Explain language, education, medicine, engineering, energy, transport and communications. Greek is a major shared language, but local languages and identities persist. Scientific development follows its own uneven history; preserving ancient scholarship does not automatically produce modern technology centuries early. Computers and modern communications may exist, but there is no AI.

Make everyday life concrete: homes, food, paid work, family, sport, theatre, music, fashion, holidays and the cost of living. Describe an ordinary resident's day in a major city and contrast it with life in a distant province. Ancient buildings coexist with modern infrastructure; this is a living society rather than a world where everyone still wears a toga.

Include present-day political disputes, separatist movements, economic pressures and environmental problems. Show how imperial nostalgia, civic pride and resentment of central power coexist. Give the world distinctive details, including its calendar, currency, public offices and modern Olympic traditions, explaining unfamiliar terms clearly.

Write from within the setting, treating its history as established reality. Distinguish official imperial mythology from historical scholarship. Invent consistent people, places, events and institutions, and link them naturally so the wiki can expand.

## Four-image plan

Include **exactly four images**: a map of the empire and neighbouring states in 2026, a modern imperial capital with ancient and contemporary architecture, a meeting of the common assembly, and daily life in a provincial city's agora.

# Oceania

## World brief

You are building a world where the events and political order of George Orwell's Nineteen Eighty-Four were real. It is now 2026. Take the lore from the book and expand it into a convincing account of how this world has developed since 1984.

The Party has survived. Forty-two more years of technological development, historical revision and psychological control have changed how it rules and how people live.

## Featured article and scope

Oceania is the main entry point into its own wiki. Treat the target as a flagship featured article of approximately **2,500–3,500 words**, with a substantial introduction, **8–10 developed sections**, useful subsections and a concluding See also section.

Write an encyclopaedic article about Oceania as it exists in 2026, with Airstrip One as a detailed example of everyday life. Establish the wider world too, including Eurasia, Eastasia, disputed territories and the changing official account of the perpetual war.

Use the book as the foundation. Preserve Ingsoc, Big Brother, the Inner Party, Outer Party and proles, the four ministries, the Thought Police, Newspeak, doublethink, telescreens, the Two Minutes Hate, unpersons and Room 101. Extend these institutions in specific, believable ways.

Include a history section with a timeline from 1984 to 2026. Draw on the technological and social changes of those decades: networked computers, the internet, mobile phones, social media, digital payments, facial recognition, drones and generative AI. Explain their equivalents in this world, who can access them, and how the Party permits or restricts them. Adapt developments such as financial crises, terrorism, pandemics and climate pressures to this alternate history rather than assuming our world's events happened unchanged.

Show how the Ministry of Truth handles digital archives, synthetic footage and the instant rewriting of millions of records. Explain how people practise doublethink when their own devices contradict what they remember.

Big Brother still appears to be alive and unchanged. Explore how his image is maintained without settling whether he was ever a real individual. Keep similar uncertainty around Goldstein, the Brotherhood and claims made in official histories.

Have sections on work, housing, food, family, sex, friendship, entertainment and religion. Give concrete examples of a normal day for an Outer Party member and a prole. Technology has advanced unevenly: surveillance can be sophisticated while homes remain cold and basic goods scarce. Explain why the regime preserves those conditions.

Describe the progress of Newspeak by 2026, including its use in education, messaging and automated language correction. Its planned replacement of Oldspeak by 2050 is still in the future.

Include resistance, private memories and attempts to preserve forbidden records. Some opposition is real and some is manufactured by the Party. Keep the atmosphere oppressive and grounded in the book's logic of power.

Write about this world from within its setting, rather than reviewing the novel or comparing it to modern politics. Distinguish official Party claims from what can actually be established. Invent consistent names, policies, incidents and institutions for the years after 1984, and link them naturally so the wiki can expand.

Don't rewrite the novel, come up with new material building on what Orwell wrote. Treat 1984 as a copyrighted book even though it's in the public domain.

## Four-image plan

Include **exactly four images**, the maximum supported. Show a London street in 2026, a domestic telescreen, the modern Ministry of Truth and everyday life in a prole district.
